The instrument

Bloom is a browser-based instrument for the Sonic Timer scenario. Instead of a numerical countdown, a lotus pond grows while you focus and the sound builds into a harmony. During the break, the pond becomes playable.

Open Bloom

Starting point

In Assignment 2 I tested several prototypes with users and chose to continue the one where notes keep arriving and build into a harmony as the session goes on. My teacher's feedback was that the project should feel more like an instrument, so for this final version I focused on making the study phase more expressive and the break phase properly playable.

Showing time through sound and growth

Instead of a countdown, time is shown by a lotus pond. Lotuses appear and slowly open, and each one adds a note to a sustained harmony. A filter on the sound gradually opens over the session, so the music brightens as the session nears its end. A sun crosses the sky and sets into the water as the break begins, so progress can be read at a glance without numbers.

Making the break an instrument

During the break, pressing a lotus sends waves across the water. When a wave reaches another lotus, that lotus chimes. Chimes get quieter the further the wave has travelled, so the pond plays a layered, gentle piece of music. Notes come from a pentatonic scale so any combination sounds pleasant together. A moon crosses the sky during the break and sets as it ends, giving the break its own progress cue.

Personalisation

Users can set their own study and break times, with Pomodoro (25/5) selected by default. A Bright or Mellow mood changes the scale, the sound and the colour palette. A Day or Night choice changes the scene: butterflies and sunshine by day, a moon, fireflies and crickets by night.

Design decisions and changes

  • The status text sits in a caption bar so it feels part of the interface.
  • All water ripples share one style, and the birth ripple starts from the centre of each lily pad.
  • I lowered the volume and pitch of the crickets after finding them annoying.

Accessibility and responsiveness

The layout has four breakpoints, visible focus outlines on controls, and it respects reduced-motion settings.

Limitations

I tested the visuals in Chromium and have not tested every browser. Audio only starts after a click because of browser rules.
