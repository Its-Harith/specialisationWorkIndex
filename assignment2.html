Assignment 2: Parallel Prototypes

Design area 1: Mapping

This area explores how a flower's place in the garden decides the note it plays. The instrument is used at the end of a study session, when the person wants to relax, so the mapping needs to make playing feel rewarding without requiring musical skill. Each prototype changes only the mapping rule.

Mapping 1 (open prototype)

Flowers appear at random positions and take random notes from a pentatonic scale. A scale where every note suits every other means any flower can be played in any order, which keeps the break low-pressure.

Mapping 2 (open prototype)

Notes are fixed to positions, running left to right, and the garden grows outward from the centre in a symmetrical layout. Because a position always gives the same note, the person can learn the layout and play the same tune again, which makes it behave more like a real instrument (closest to a kalimba).

Mapping 3 (open prototype)

Flowers are grouped into three clusters, each with its own chord. Moving within a cluster stays harmonious, and moving between clusters changes the harmony, so the relationship between flowers carries the meaning and no single flower does.

Design area 2: Information Design

This area explores how the passing of study time is communicated, since the instrument replaces a countdown timer. The garden's growth is the timer, and each prototype changes the pattern of that growth.

Information Design 1 (open prototype)

Flowers appear at an even pace while a bar fills at the same constant rate, behind a blur. At 100% the bar bursts and the blur lifts, which marks the switch from waiting to playing as a single clear moment. It acts as the baseline the other two are compared against.

Information Design 2 (open prototype)

Flowers appear more and more quickly towards the end, while a bar along the top still advances steadily. This separates real elapsed time from perceived pace, in line with how focus sessions often feel slow at the start and urgent near the end.

Commit logs

Information Design 3 (open prototype)

Each flower adds a sustained note at its own pitch, so a harmony builds as the session goes on and progress can be heard as well as seen. This lets the person keep track of time without looking at the screen, which suits focused work.
